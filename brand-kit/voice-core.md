# Gratitude.com: Voice Core

Gratitude.com tells a human story supported by a system, not a systems story with humans added to it. The voice is plain, warm, and confident: it makes something real feel close enough to act on right now. It sounds like good that is already funded and ready, waiting for a person to say "Yes. Make this happen."

## Personality Traits

**Human first**: Design for feeling, not just understanding. A meal should feel like a meal reaching someone. An acknowledgment should feel like one human being genuinely seeing another.
**Plain-spoken**: Plain language over system language. "It is funded; your choice gives the go-ahead."
**Action-oriented**: Gratitude becomes meaningful when it moves. Every line points toward one clear action.
**Confident, never hyped**: Trust comes from visible outcomes and real status, not generic claims.
**Respectful**: Of participants, partners, and the people who receive the good. No pity, no guilt, no virtue signaling.

## Tone by Context

| Context | Tone |
|---------|------|
| Participant (activator) copy | Warm, clear, agency: possibility, then confidence |
| Funder communications | Generous and concrete; the funder deserves a result loop too; no guilt |
| Sponsoring organization comms | Strategic, credibility-forward, subtle about attribution |
| Nonprofit partner comms | Respectful, collaborative, dignity-first |
| Website copy | One brand promise, one explanation, one action |
| Status and impact updates | Human, not package tracking or enterprise software |
| Social media and share objects | Share the impact, not the ego |
| Impact reports | Data-forward, credibility-first (metrics only when verified) |
| Investor materials | See investor-core.yaml: credibility and numbers, declarative |

## Voice Rules

- **No em dashes, anywhere.** Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead. This applies to copy, examples, headlines, and quotes from source documents.
- Short sentences preferred over long compound sentences
- Plain language over system language; consumers should not need to understand funding pools or nonprofit operations to act
- No excessive qualifiers ("maybe," "sort of," "quite," "potentially")
- Never over-promise; let verified outcomes speak. No invented numbers.
- Lead with the act and the result, back with how the system works
- Activate is dominant, Fund is secondary, Share is tertiary. Avoid three equally loud CTAs.
- Invitation, not conversion funnel. Return comes from impact itself, not points, streaks, or badges.
- Never guilt, never pity-based storytelling, never imply personal rescue or ask the reader to decide who deserves help
- Never make the Commitment sound like a required pledge
- Keep MVP copy in the NOW stage: no Express, Tell Them, or message-first language unless the piece is explicitly about what comes NEXT
- Sponsor and nonprofit names never overpower the act or the participant's choice
- US spelling: "acknowledgment"
- Use "we" in platform context
